# Publication Misconduct Taxonomy

Authoritative sources: COPE (Committee on Publication Ethics), ORI (Office of Research Integrity),
ICMJE (International Committee of Medical Journal Editors), Retraction Watch Database.

---

## 1. Duplicate Publication (also: Redundant Publication)

**Definition**: Publishing the same data or conclusions in two or more papers without cross-reference
or disclosure, giving the impression of more findings than actually exist.

**Signals to detect**:
- Abstract similarity ≥ 80% verbatim between two papers by same authors
- Identical tables, figures, or data described in prose
- Same sample sizes, effect sizes, or p-values appearing in multiple papers
- Title nearly identical with trivial word changes

**Legitimate exceptions** (NOT misconduct):
- Preprint → peer-reviewed journal (standard practice; preprint should be cited)
- Conference paper → extended journal version (must disclose; overlap should be <30%)
- Translation into another language (must disclose in both)
- Systematic review with different inclusion criteria than prior reviews

**COPE position**: Even when properly disclosed, editors should be notified before submission.

---

## 2. Salami Slicing (also: Least Publishable Unit)

**Definition**: Artificially dividing one study into multiple papers to maximize publication count,
each of which alone would be insufficient for publication.

**Signals to detect**:
- Same cohort/dataset appearing in 3+ papers from same group in same period
- Papers with sequential titles suggesting a split narrative ("Part I", "Part II", "Study A", etc.)
- Sample sizes described as subsets of a previously published larger cohort
- Same baseline characteristics table appearing in multiple papers
- Methodology section nearly identical across papers but outcomes differ

**Legitimate exception**: Multi-paper series with distinct research questions, clearly disclosed as
a series and cross-referenced.

---

## 3. Self-Plagiarism (also: Text Recycling)

**Definition**: Reusing one's own previously published text without attribution, particularly in
introduction and discussion sections.

**Signals to detect**:
- Introduction paragraphs with ≥3 consecutive sentences appearing in prior paper
- Methods section copied verbatim from prior paper (more borderline — methods often must be
  repeated accurately)
- Identical or near-identical discussion/conclusion paragraphs

**Context matters**:
- Methods reuse is more acceptable than results/discussion reuse
- Unpublished PhD thesis material used in papers: generally acceptable
- COPE notes: text recycling of methods is tolerated by many journals if data is new

---

## 4. Plagiarism of Others' Work

**Definition**: Using another researcher's text, ideas, or data without attribution.

**Signals to detect**:
- High abstract similarity (≥50%) between two papers from **different** author groups
- No citation to the source paper despite near-identical framing
- Identical hypothesis/framing without citation

---

## 5. Dual/Simultaneous Submission

**Definition**: Submitting the same manuscript to two journals at the same time.

**Signals to detect**:
- Same paper appears in two journals with close publication dates (within 3 months)
- Two papers with identical data/conclusions published in same year without mutual citation
- Preprint timestamp significantly predates publication (normal) vs. two simultaneous publications

**Note**: Cannot be definitively detected from text alone; requires journal metadata comparison.

---

## 6. Honorary / Ghost Authorship

**Definition**:
- **Honorary (gift) authorship**: Including someone as author who did not contribute
- **Ghost authorship**: Excluding someone who made substantial contributions

**Signals to detect**:
- Author list changes dramatically between preprint and published version
- First author not affiliated with institution conducting the research
- Senior author appears on many papers across unrelated topics (honorific authorship)

**Note**: Difficult to detect without institutional knowledge. Flag unusual authorship patterns.

---

## 7. Predatory Publishing

**Definition**: Journals that charge fees without providing legitimate peer review or editorial
services, often accepting any paper for payment.

**Signals to detect**:
- Journal not indexed in PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, or DOAJ
- Journal name mimics legitimate journals (slight spelling variation)
- Very short time from submission to acceptance (< 2 weeks)
- Journal has no impact factor or recent citations
- Publisher appears on Beall's List or similar resources

**Check**: DOAJ, Sherpa/Romeo, PubMed journal list, Scopus journal list.

---

## 8. Data Fabrication / Manipulation

**Beyond scope of text similarity**: Requires data analysis. However, flag if:
- Paper reports results identical to a prior retracted paper
- Numeric data in tables exactly matches another paper with different reported methodology
- Statistical anomalies (suspiciously consistent effect sizes across papers)

---

## Severity Classification Summary

| Type | Severity | Primary Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Verbatim duplicate (same authors) | 🔴 Critical | Abstract ≥80% match, same authors |
| Duplicate (different authors, no citation) | 🔴 Critical | High abstract overlap, no citation |
| Salami slicing | 🔴 High | Same dataset, multiple fragmented papers |
| Self-plagiarism (introduction/discussion) | 🟡 Moderate | Text overlap ≥50%, same author |
| Methods reuse | 🟢 Low | Normal if new data presented |
| Preprint → journal | 🟢 Normal | Expected; check for proper citation |
| Independent parallel research | ℹ️ Info | High topic similarity, different teams |
| Extended conference → journal | 🟢 Low | Acceptable with disclosure |

---

## Recommended Actions by Severity

**🔴 Critical**: Recommend user contact journal editors and/or institutional research integrity
officer. Point to COPE guidelines (publicationethics.org). Do not make final judgment — present
evidence and let user/institution decide.

**🟡 Moderate**: Recommend user review the overlapping sections and add citations or
differentiation before submission. Suggest checking journal's self-plagiarism policy.

**🟢 Low / Normal**: Note the finding for completeness; no action required.

**ℹ️ Info**: Useful background on the research landscape; no misconduct implied.
